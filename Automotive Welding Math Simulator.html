About these models
  • These are teaching models built on the three equations above. Each run is evaluated at 400 points along its own time axis.
  • Spot: k is set so that 9.2 kA for 200 ms on 2 mm of mild steel gives the 5.0 mm standard nugget. R follows a dynamic-resistance curve that falls as the contact softens. Latent heat is ignored.
  • Edge fillet joint: the upper sheet (1.0 mm) edge sits on the lower sheet (1.2 mm). The weld is made in the corner, and penetration is measured from the sheet interface down into the lower sheet. The minimum required is 0.3 mm. Reaching the lower sheet thickness means burn-through.
  • Growth: the shown depth is the root-mean-square of the equation value accumulated over the weld time, a teaching assumption that makes depth grow until the end, like the spot nugget.
  • GMAW: K is an illustrative value for each wire diameter (thin-sheet range). It must be calibrated by experiment for a real material.
  • Laser: material properties are typical handbook values. The fitted equation is only reliable near the conditions it was fitted on, so very low speeds or soft materials can give unrealistic depths. A calibration factor C (aluminium 0.25) pulls the aluminium result back to a realistic range.
  • Time profiles (ramps, dips, speed variation) are examples that show how a changing input changes the output.
